# Module 5: Communicating Patient Deterioration & Escalation Protocols

**Emergency Nursing Communication Course**  
**Level: A2 (Upper-Intermediate)**  
**Total Duration: ~2 hours 17 minutes across 8 lessons**

---

## 📋 Module Overview

This module equips nurses with critical communication skills for recognizing, assessing, and escalating patient deterioration in high-pressure emergency settings. It bridges the gap between clinical assessment and professional communication, emphasizing assertive, structured escalation using the SBAR framework.

### Learning Outcomes

By the end of this module, nurses will be able to:

1. **Recognize vital sign deterioration** — Understand the clinical significance of SpO2 drops, BP spikes, HR elevation, and consciousness changes
2. **Escalate effectively using SBAR** — Structure urgent information for doctors, nurses, and team members
3. **Communicate under pressure** — Stay calm and articulate complex clinical scenarios during emergencies
4. **Handle family concerns** — Reassure anxious family members while being honest about patient condition changes
5. **Document and hand over cases** — Provide clear, sequential summaries of deteriorating patient journeys
6. **Demonstrate assertiveness** — Speak up when a patient needs help, without panic or uncertainty
7. **Balance urgency and professionalism** — Convey seriousness without causing unnecessary alarm
8. **Self-evaluate communication quality** — Reflect on how effectively they communicated under stress

## 🎯 Module Progression

### **Lesson 1-2: HEADS UP** (Exposure & Noticing)
Focus: **Learn the language of deterioration**

#### Lesson 1: *Vital Signs in Crisis — What the Numbers Mean*
- **Duration:** 12 minutes
- **Steps:**
  1. **Scenario Intro** — A patient's vital signs deteriorate (SpO2 drop, HR spike, confusion)
  2. **Audio Shadow** — Listen to expert nurses describing vital sign changes with clinical detachment
  3. **Script Read** — Color-coded dialogue: Nurse explaining deterioration to alert patient
  4. **Quiz** — Understanding SpO2 <90%, consciousness changes, what needs escalation

**Key Phrases Introduced:**
- "His oxygen saturation is dropping"
- "SpO2 is now 87 percent"
- "Heart rate has jumped to 128"
- "He's becoming increasingly confused"
- "Level of consciousness dropping"

---

#### Lesson 2: *Key Phrases in Action — Red Flags & Urgency*
- **Duration:** 12 minutes
- **Steps:**
  1. **Audio Shadow** — Red-flag language: "I'm concerned about deterioration," "This is urgent," "We need help NOW"
  2. **Script Read** — Nurse escalating to doctor with structured language
  3. **Cloze** — Fill in critical escalation phrases
  4. **Quiz** — Identify appropriate urgency levels

**Key Phrases Introduced:**
- "I'm concerned about deterioration"
- "This is urgent"
- "We need help NOW"
- "Patient is in acute distress"
- "Immediate intervention required"
- "Can you come to bed 7 immediately?"

---

### **Lesson 3-5: HEADS DOWN** (Controlled Practice)
Focus: **Respond to deterioration scenarios; use structured language**

#### Lesson 3: *Understanding the Situation — Controlled Practice*
- **Duration:** 15 minutes
- **Steps:**
  1. **Audio Shadow** — Scenario: SpO2 drops during sleep; nurses assess and respond
  2. **Cloze** — Complete clinical observations
  3. **No Script** — Speak freely: "What would you say to the team?"
  4. **Quiz** — Understanding gradual vs. sudden deterioration

**Scenario:** SpO2 falls from 96% to 89% while patient is sleeping. Nurse must wake patient, reposition, increase oxygen flow.

**Key Clinical Decision:** Gradual decline allows intervention time; sudden drops demand immediate action.

---

#### Lesson 4: *A Second Scenario — Blood Pressure Crisis*
- **Duration:** 15 minutes
- **Steps:**
  1. **Scenario Intro** — Post-op patient: BP rises from 120/75 to 165/95; patient pale, agitated
  2. **Audio Shadow** — Nurse reporting hypertensive crisis; suspects pain or medication issue
  3. **Cloze** — Complete the clinical report with vital sign numbers and assessment
  4. **Recording Submit** — Call a doctor; escalate the BP crisis

**Scenario:** Recovery room patient suddenly becomes pale and restless. BP spiking to 165/95. Possible post-op hypertension or inadequate pain control.

**Key Phrases:**
- "Blood pressure is rising rapidly"
- "Systolic 165, diastolic 95"
- "He's pale and agitated"
- "I suspect post-operative hypertension or pain response"
- "We need to review his medications immediately"

---

#### Lesson 5: *Your Turn to Speak — Open Deterioration Scenario*
- **Duration:** 18 minutes
- **Steps:**
  1. **Scenario Intro** — Complex multi-system deterioration: SpO2 ↓, HR ↑, BP ↑, confusion, respiratory distress
  2. **Cloze** — Organize your assessment using clinical language
  3. **No Script** — Prepare 90-second message to call for help (use SBAR if possible)
  4. **Recording Submit** — Record your escalation call (120 seconds); rubric evaluates SBAR, clinical language, urgency, specificity

**Scenario:** 65-year-old patient shows multiple vital changes:
- SpO2: 94% → 88%
- HR: normal → 115
- BP: 120/70 → 140/88
- Mental state: alert → increasingly confused
- Breathing: normal → labored

**Student Task:** Assess and escalate within 2 minutes.

---

### **Lesson 6-7: HEADS TOGETHER** (Pair Practice)
Focus: **Role-play escalation with peers; navigate different relationships**

#### Lesson 6: *Pair Practice — Round 1: Structured Escalation*
- **Duration:** 20 minutes
- **Steps:**
  1. **Script Read** — Full dialogue: Junior nurse → Senior nurse escalation
  2. **Cloze** — Practice key phrases from the dialogue
  3. **No Script** — Free escalation practice (60 seconds)
  4. **Recording Submit** — Record your escalation; peer will respond

**Dialogue Scenario:**
- **Junior Nurse:** "I'm concerned about deterioration in bed 5. SpO2 dropped from 95 to 87. HR is 118 and rising. Patient is confused."
- **Senior Nurse:** "I'm coming now. While I'm on my way, prepare for ICU transfer. Get a blood gas sample. Notify the doctor. Keep monitoring every 2 minutes."

**Key Learning:** How to hand off deteriorating patients to colleagues; what information is critical vs. nice-to-have.

---

#### Lesson 7: *Pair Practice — Round 2: Responding to Family Anxiety*
- **Duration:** 20 minutes
- **Steps:**
  1. **Script Read** — Nurse ↔ Distressed family dialogue
  2. **Cloze** — Empathetic and reassuring language
  3. **No Script** — Free speaking: Reassure a frightened family member (90 seconds)
  4. **Recording Submit** — Record your family communication

**Dialogue Scenario:**
- **Family Member:** "Oh no! What does that mean? Is she going to die? Why did this happen?"
- **Nurse:** "I need to be honest with you — her condition has changed. Her oxygen levels have dropped. We're investigating why and giving her more oxygen. The doctor is on the way. We catch these changes early so we can act."

**Key Learning:** How to balance honesty with reassurance; avoid jargon; acknowledge fear without dismissing concerns.

---

### **Lesson 8: ASSESSMENT** (Mixed Input + Self-Reflection)
Focus: **Comprehensive exam; metacognitive reflection on communication**

#### Lesson 8: *Module Assessment — Mixed Input & Self-Reflection*
- **Duration:** 25 minutes
- **Steps:**
  1. **Quiz** — 4 mixed-format questions (MCQ, listening, matching)
     - Critical decision-making under time pressure
     - SBAR framework knowledge
     - Clinical significance of vital signs
  2. **Cloze** — Complete a full SBAR handover (Situation, Background, Assessment, Recommendation)
  3. **Recording Submit** — **FINAL ASSESSMENT:** Full escalation call (150 seconds)
     - Scenario: COPD exacerbation patient deteriorating (SpO2 88, HR 118, BP 145/88, confused, restless)
     - Rubric evaluates SBAR structure, vital sign accuracy, clinical language, urgency appropriateness, action-oriented recommendations
  4. **Self-Reflection** — 5 emoji-scale questions + open text
     - Clarity of explanations
     - Confidence under pressure
     - Completeness of communication
     - Urgency balance (avoiding panic while conveying seriousness)
     - Clinical language use
     - Open: "What was your biggest challenge? What do you want to practice more?"

## 🗣️ Key Phrases & Vocabulary Framework

### **Vital Sign Language**

#### SpO2 (Oxygen Saturation)
```
His oxygen saturation is dropping.
SpO2 has fallen to 88 percent.
We're seeing a gradual desaturation.
Oxygen levels are critical — we need immediate intervention.
```

#### Blood Pressure
```
Blood pressure is rising rapidly.
BP spiking — systolic 160, diastolic 95.
Hypotensive — 90 over 50, and dropping.
We've lost BP — patient is unresponsive.
```

#### Heart Rate
```
Heart rate is elevated at 125.
Pulse is extremely weak and rapid.
ECG showing irregular rhythm.
Tachycardia — rate over 140.
```

#### Consciousness & Behavior
```
Patient is becoming increasingly confused.
Level of consciousness dropping.
He's agitated and not responding to commands.
She's unresponsive to verbal stimuli.
```

---

### **Red-Flag Language**
```
This is urgent.
I'm concerned about deterioration.
We need help NOW.
This patient is critically unstable.
Immediate intervention required.
Patient is in acute distress.
Critical situation developing.
We may lose this patient.
```

---

### **Escalation Phrases**
```
Can you come to bed 7 immediately?
I need a doctor to review this patient right now.
Please respond code blue.
We have a medical emergency.
Doctor, I'm escalating this case.
This is beyond my scope — I need senior support.
I'm calling a rapid response team.
Prepare for emergency transfer to ICU.
```

---

### **SBAR Framework**

#### **S**ituation
```
I'm calling about patient in bed 7.
The situation is that my patient's condition has changed.
This started approximately 20 minutes ago.
```

#### **B**ackground
```
He was admitted with pneumonia 3 days ago.
He's on oxygen therapy and antibiotics.
Vital signs were stable until an hour ago.
```

#### **A**ssessment
```
My assessment is that he's deteriorating.
SpO2 has dropped from 96 to 87 percent.
He's more confused and his breathing is labored.
I think he may be going into respiratory failure.
```

#### **R**ecommendation
```
I recommend we move him to ICU immediately.
Could you order a blood gas analysis?
I think we need to increase his oxygen support.
Should we prepare for intubation?
```

---

### **Family Communication Language**
```
Your mother's condition has changed.
We're monitoring her very carefully right now.
I want to be honest with you — we're concerned.
We're taking steps to keep her safe.
The doctor is coming to talk with you shortly.
This doesn't mean the worst has happened.
We catch these changes early so we can act.
I know this is frightening. We're doing everything we can.
```
